2. Executive summary

Bottom line

Proposed recommendation: Do not proceed at the current price of US$24.50 per share. Pause, and re-engage only on a re-priced and re-structured basis. The Investment Committee makes the decision.

The data room does not support the price. Two payments that this transaction itself triggers, the Pittock pension funding (230.5) and the Nimbus earn-out (90.0), total 320.5 (20.8% of EV) and are missing from the model. Deducting only those two items from management's own numbers takes implied value to about US$18.38 per share, below the US$18.70 unaffected price (X-018). On adjusted earnings and the full debt-like bridge, implied value is about US$12.73 per share, and lower again once the lost WSEA contract is removed (projection, X-018).

Top five red flags

  1. The change of control forces 230.5 of pension funding within 120 days (X-001, Critical). Under the 2019 Pittock trust agreement, a change of control requires funding on a plan termination basis. Termination liabilities of about 522.0 against assets of 291.5 give a shortfall of 230.5 (14.9% of EV). The model assumes no pension adjustment.
  2. The largest customer has gone (X-003, Critical). WSEA (rank 1, 96.0, 12.2% of FY2025 revenue) awarded its 2027 to 2031 program to Thermiq on July 21, 2026. Delmora's contract ends December 31, 2026, before the planned close. The Q2 10-Q filed 16 days after the award still says "We expect to be selected".
  3. The earnings base is overstated (X-004, Critical). LTM Adjusted EBITDA is about 115.4, not 130.4, after removing the Aura and Calder add-backs and normalizing a warranty accrual cut. The effect is 177.5 at the deal multiple (11.5% of EV). This is just over the 10% line and rests on a 5.8 warranty estimate.
  4. Core technology and data rights are contested (X-009, X-008, X-010, High). Calder claims ownership of all six AdaptiveComfort patents, with trial on February 8, 2027. Delmora's public defense conflicts with its own 10-K filing date. The named inventor and former CTO has left and has separate counsel. Insights, to which the deal team attributes about 310 of EV, rests on opt-out consent while a 2022 FTC order requires express consent for new uses, and the Colorado AG is suing.
  5. Contingent liabilities the brief calls contained or immaterial are large (X-011, X-012, X-013, X-007, SEC-023, High). CPSC staff want a refund or replacement for 1.62m Aura units (up to 192.8 at full refund uptake), and the claims appear to fall within an insurance exclusion. Off-site groundwater contamination has been confirmed at the former Pittock plant. The IRS proposes 121.1 of tax and penalty against a 14.2 reserve. BIS has subpoenaed records on the Middle East distributor behind most FY2025 growth. The EC has issued a Statement of Objections (fine cap 78.6).

  • Dismissed litigation: the Brennan Sales Group commission claim (3.2) was dismissed with prejudice on January 22, 2026 (LIT-007, SEC-034).

3. Answers to the six IC questions

Q1. Quality of earnings and cash flow

No. Neither the 130.4 of LTM Adjusted EBITDA nor the 134.1 of H1 2026 operating cash flow is a sound base.

  • Earnings: removing the Aura field action (6.0, a product quality cost while warranty claims doubled) and the Calder legal fees (3.2, recurring until the case ends) gives 121.2. Normalizing the Q4 2025 warranty accrual cut to the FY2024 rate removes a further 5.8, giving about 115.4 (X-004; FIN-001 to FIN-004, SEC-015). Add-backs are 23.2% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA. Stock-based compensation (17.6) is left in, as market practice (FIN-006).
  • Forward base: the WSEA loss (X-003), the unhedged peso (17.0 to 18.7 a year not yet in LTM) and the gross margin fall to 39.4% (X-016) all reduce earnings further.
  • Cash flow: 98.0 of the 134.1 is timing. Delmora moved its main contract manufacturer from 60 to 120 day terms from April 1, 2026 (64.0), and received a 34.0 WSEA advance for H2 2026 incentives. Underlying H1 2026 operating cash flow is 36.1, in line with H1 2025 (34.9) (X-005). DPO rose from 78 to 121 days.
  • Adjustments needed: rebase EBITDA to about 115.4 before WSEA; treat 57.1 of excess payables and the 34.0 advance as debt-like; set a working capital peg (X-005, X-018).

Q3. Technology and data rights

Not established. Delmora's ownership of AdaptiveComfort and its right to use customer data as the growth plan assumes are both contested.

  • AdaptiveComfort title (X-009, High). Calder claims all six patents, assignment, and a 4% royalty on thermostat net sales since 2019, and says it would license other manufacturers. The earliest provisional application was filed on March 6, 2017, during Dr. Reyes-Barrow's Calder fellowship (September 2014 to June 2017). Delmora's spokesperson says the algorithm was conceived after June 2017, which conflicts with that filing date (inference). The court denied Delmora's motion for judgment on the pleadings on limitations because the defense "turns on disputed facts"; the defense remains open. Trial is set for February 8, 2027. The royalty upper bound is 20.2 a year on LTM Devices revenue (FIN-051); no source splits thermostats from sensors and hubs.
  • Key person (X-010, High). The named inventor gave notice on July 27, 2026, three days before that ruling, and has had separate counsel since August 20. The 8-K says there was "no disagreement" and does not mention Calder.
  • Open source (LIT-006, Medium). The Open Firmware Conservancy sued on September 10, 2026 under GPLv2, and says the ac-runtime AdaptiveComfort code must be released (allegation, NEWS-009).
  • Insights data (X-008, High). Insights enrolls subscribers by opt-out under updated terms. The 2022 FTC order requires affirmative express consent for sharing covered information beyond the purposes disclosed at collection. Delmora calls the data "aggregated and de-identified". The Ledger identified the household behind 61% of records in a test area. The Colorado AG seeks an opt-in injunction for Colorado households, and its complaint states the FTC is investigating order compliance (allegation). About 310 of EV (20.1%) is attributed to Insights; no source sizes the loss.
  • Tax on the same IP (X-013). The IRS says the 2019 transfer of the same rights to Ireland was undervalued by 412.0.

OEM licensing (thesis point 2) should not be valued until title and consent are resolved.

Q4. Regulatory, trade and legal exposure

Matters that could delay or block closing or impair the business:

  • Export controls (X-007, High). BIS subpoenaed records on June 24, 2026 about shipments to a UAE distributor, and Delmora suspended shipments to it. Gulf Horizon Trading FZE (Dubai, rank 4, 38.6) supplied 71.9% of FY2025 revenue growth; that it is the subpoenaed distributor is an inference. A trade newsletter reported diversion to Iran from two anonymous former employees (rumor). The Audit Committee held special trade compliance meetings on January 20, February 9 and February 23, 2026, before the 10-K said "We are not aware of any violations".
  • Product safety (X-011, High). CPSC staff (letter dated August 28, 2026) made a preliminary substantial-hazard finding on 1.62m Aura units and seek a refund or replacement. Incident counts rose from 214 reports, 37 fires and 9 burn injuries (10-Q) to 296, 52 and 11. A consolidated class action is pending (motion to dismiss hearing November 18, 2026). The insurer has reserved all rights.
  • Privacy (X-008, High): Colorado AG suit (answer due October 27, 2026) and possible FTC order action.
  • IP (X-009, High): Calder trial February 8, 2027, inside the expected closing window.
  • Competition (SEC-023, High): EC Statement of Objections of June 30, 2026 on cross-border online sales restrictions; reply due October 30, 2026; fines up to 10% of turnover (cap 78.6).
  • Environmental (X-012, High): Ohio EPA sampling at about 60 homes due November 30, 2026 and a plume plan by December 15, 2026.
  • Tax (X-013, High): IRS NOPA under appeal.
  • Labor (SEC-030, Medium): the Reno CBA expired September 30, 2026 and 91% of votes cast authorized a strike; the contract has a successorship clause.
  • Shareholder vote (NEWS-021, Medium): Tarnwood (6.2%) opposes US$24.50 and seeks at least US$30.

None is a merger-control issue, but the BIS matter and the Calder trial could each delay closing, and several dates fall between signing (November 30) and the planned Q1 2027 close.

Weaker than the thesis assumes (X-015, High).

  • Utility Programs: WSEA (96.0, 68.1% of the segment) ends December 31, 2026 and is lost (X-003). Segment growth slowed from 16.7% (FY2023) to 4.3% (H1 2026), below the 6% model assumption (FIN-022). Without WSEA the segment base is about 45.0; Utility Programs excluding WSEA did grow 12.5% in FY2025 (FIN-047).

  • Together, WSEA and Gulf Horizon are 134.6 (17.1% of FY2025 revenue) at risk.
