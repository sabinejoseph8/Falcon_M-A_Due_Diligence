## 2. Executive summary

### Bottom line

**Proposed recommendation: Do not proceed at the current price of US$24.50 per share. Pause, and re-engage only on a re-priced and re-structured basis.** The Investment Committee makes the decision.

The data room does not support the price. Two payments that this transaction itself triggers, the Pittock pension funding (230.5) and the Nimbus earn-out (90.0), total 320.5 (20.8% of EV) and are missing from the model. Deducting only those two items from management's own numbers takes implied value to about US$18.38 per share, below the US$18.70 unaffected price (X-018). ### Top five red flags

1. **The change of control forces 230.5 of pension funding within 120 days** (X-001, Critical). Under the 2019 Pittock trust agreement, a change of control requires funding on a plan termination basis. Termination liabilities of about 522.0 against assets of 291.5 give a shortfall of 230.5 (14.9% of EV). The model assumes no pension adjustment.
2. **The largest customer has gone** (X-003, Critical). WSEA (rank 1, 96.0, 12.2% of FY2025 revenue) awarded its 2027 to 2031 program to Thermiq on July 21, 2026. Delmora's contract ends December 31, 2026, before the planned close. The Q2 10-Q filed 16 days after the award still says "We expect to be selected".
3. **The earnings base is overstated** (X-004, Critical). LTM Adjusted EBITDA is about 115.4, not 130.4, after removing the Aura and Calder add-backs and normalizing a warranty accrual cut. The effect is 177.5 at the deal multiple (11.5% of EV). This is just over the 10% line and rests on a 5.8 warranty estimate.
4. **Core technology and data rights are contested** (X-009, X-008, X-010, High). Calder claims ownership of all six AdaptiveComfort patents, with trial on February 8, 2027. Insights, to which the deal team attributes about 310 of EV, rests on opt-out consent while a 2022 FTC order requires express consent for new uses, and the Colorado AG is suing.

CPSC staff want a refund or replacement for 1.62m Aura units (up to 192.8 at full refund uptake), and the claims appear to fall within an insurance exclusion. Off-site groundwater contamination has been confirmed at the former Pittock plant. The IRS proposes 121.1 of tax and penalty against a 14.2 reserve. BIS has subpoenaed records on the Middle East distributor behind most FY2025 growth. The EC has issued a Statement of Objections (fine cap 78.6).

**No. Neither the 130.4 of LTM Adjusted EBITDA nor the 134.1 of H1 2026 operating cash flow is a sound base.**

Underlying H1 2026 operating cash flow is 36.1, in line with H1 2025 (34.9) (X-005). DPO rose from 78 to 121 days.

### Q3. Technology and data rights

**Not established.** Delmora's ownership of AdaptiveComfort and its right to use customer data as the growth plan assumes are both contested.

- **AdaptiveComfort title (X-009, High).** Calder claims all six patents, assignment, and a 4% royalty on thermostat net sales since 2019, and says it would license other manufacturers. The earliest provisional application was filed on March 6, 2017, during Dr. Reyes-Barrow's Calder fellowship (September 2014 to June 2017). Delmora's spokesperson says the algorithm was conceived after June 2017, which conflicts with that filing date (inference). The court denied Delmora's motion for judgment on the pleadings on limitations because the defense "turns on disputed facts"; the defense remains open. Trial is set for February 8, 2027. The royalty upper bound is 20.2 a year on LTM Devices revenue (FIN-051); no source splits thermostats from sensors and hubs.
- **Key person (X-010, High).** The named inventor gave notice on July 27, 2026, three days before that ruling, and has had separate counsel since August 20. The 8-K says there was "no disagreement" and does not mention Calder.
- **Open source (LIT-006, Medium).** The Open Firmware Conservancy sued on September 10, 2026 under GPLv2, and says the `ac-runtime` AdaptiveComfort code must be released (allegation, NEWS-009).
- **Insights data (X-008, High).** Insights enrolls subscribers by opt-out under updated terms. The 2022 FTC order requires affirmative express consent for sharing covered information beyond the purposes disclosed at collection. Delmora calls the data "aggregated and de-identified". The Ledger identified the household behind 61% of records in a test area. The Colorado AG seeks an opt-in injunction for Colorado households, and its complaint states the FTC is investigating order compliance (allegation). About 310 of EV (20.1%) is attributed to Insights; no source sizes the loss.
- **Tax on the same IP (X-013).** The IRS says the 2019 transfer of the same rights to Ireland was undervalued by 412.0.

OEM licensing (thesis point 2) should not be valued until title and consent are resolved.
