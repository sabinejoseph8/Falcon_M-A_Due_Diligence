### Findings

#### NEWS-001: CPSC staff push for refund or replacement of 1.62 million Aura thermostats
- **Finding:** CPSC staff made a preliminary finding that Aura AT-200 and AT-210 units are a substantial product hazard. They want a recall offering a full refund or a free replacement, at the owner's choice. If Delmora refuses, staff may recommend an administrative complaint. If every owner of the 1.62m units chose a refund at the $119 average price, the cost would be about US$192.8m (1.62m x $119). That is 32 times the US$6.0m reserve in the deal brief, 12.5% of the US$1,543.4m enterprise value, and about $3.68 per share. Actual uptake would be lower, but no uptake figure is in the news coverage.
- **Evidence:** "a full refund of the purchase price or a free replacement thermostat"; "about 1.62 million affected units were sold, at an average retail price of $119".
- **Source:** `targets/heron/data/news/2026-09-08_consumer-safety-wire_cpsc-refund-push.md`, paragraphs 2 to 4 (Consumer Safety Wire, September 8, 2026; letter dated August 28, 2026).
- **Claim type:** Fact (reported contents of a regulator's letter); the staff position is preliminary.
- **Severity:** Critical. The test is change in value by more than 10%: the full-uptake refund gross of US$192.8m equals 12.5% of EV. It also contradicts the deal brief's "contained" and "fully reserved" assumption.
- **Confidence:** Medium. A single trade outlet reports a letter it has seen, with precise figures. Delmora's response ("disagrees") implicitly confirms the letter exists.
- **Deal impact:** Valuation (reserve shortfall), closing risk (a possible MAE debate), reputation.
- **Cross-check:** SEC 10-Q and 8-K filings after August 28 for disclosure of the CPSC letter and the reserve level; the warranty and recall reserve rollforward in the financials; any CPSC docket in the litigation and regulatory folder; Aura unit sales by model in the financial data.

- **Evidence:** "statically linked to a modified version of libthermctl ... must be distributed under GPLv2 with its source code."

- **Claim type:** Allegation (interested advocacy group).

- **Confidence:** Low. The legal interpretation is untested and the source is a single interested party.
- **Deal impact:** Technology and IP rights, post-closing engineering cost.
- **Cross-check:** Any suit by OFC in the litigation records; open source risk factors in the filings; the code scan in technical diligence (not in data room).
