## 1. The opportunity

Halvard Ridge Capital Partners Fund IV ("Halvard", a private equity fund) is considering a take-private of **Corvane Diagnostics, Inc.** (NASDAQ: CRVD, "Corvane"), a clinical laboratory company based in Raleigh, North Carolina.

Corvane has three segments:

- **Clinical testing:** genetic and molecular tests ordered by physicians and paid by Medicare, Medicaid and commercial health plans. The flagship product is the OncoTrace hereditary cancer panel.
- **Biopharma services:** central laboratory and biomarker testing for drug companies running clinical trials.
- **Lab informatics:** subscription software that hospital laboratories use to manage orders and results.

Corvane runs seven CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited laboratories and has about 2,100 employees.

## 5. Investment thesis

1. Steady growth in genetic testing, with Corvane growing revenue 11.7% in FY2025.
2. Margin expansion from lab automation and the new billing system.
3. A growing biopharma services backlog (US$246.0 million at year end 2025).
4. Add-on acquisitions of regional pathology labs.

## 6. Key model assumptions (base case, model built as of June 30, 2026)

- Starting point: FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of US$125.3 million as reported by Corvane, with all company add-backs accepted.
- FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of US$137.0 million, the midpoint of Corvane's FY2026 guidance of US$134 to 140 million.
- Revenue growth of 9% a year from 2027 to 2030.
- Medicare reimbursement rates flat through 2030.
- The biopharma services backlog converts to revenue on its historical schedule, including the Veltrane Therapeutics programs.
- No material litigation or regulatory costs beyond those described in the FY2025 10-K.

## 7. Questions for the Investment Committee

1. **Quality of earnings:** Are Corvane's revenue and Adjusted EBITDA real, recurring and large enough to support the valuation and a term loan sized at 6.0x Adjusted EBITDA?
2. **Reimbursement and compliance:** How exposed is Corvane to Medicare pricing, payer behavior and healthcare fraud and abuse risk, and what could that do to value?
3. **Legal and regulatory:** Which legal or regulatory matters could block or delay closing, change value, or need a specific protection?
4. **Capital structure:** Is Corvane's balance sheet stable between now and closing, including covenants, liquidity and change-of-control provisions?
5. **Disclosure, controls and governance:** Are Corvane's disclosures, internal controls, management incentives and related-party arrangements reliable?
6. **Price and structure:** Does US$24.00 per share still make sense, and what price changes, conditions, escrows or indemnities should we require?
