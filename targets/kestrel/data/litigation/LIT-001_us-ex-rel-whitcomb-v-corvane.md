# United States ex rel. Whitcomb v. Corvane Diagnostics, Inc.

> FICTIONAL. This case, the parties and the court record are invented for a portfolio demonstration.

**Court:** U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of North Carolina
**Case number:** 5:24-cv-00187
**Type:** False Claims Act (qui tam), with Anti-Kickback Statute theory
**Relator:** Dana Whitcomb, former Regional Sales Director (Southeast), Corvane Diagnostics, employed 2017 to 2023
**Defendant:** Corvane Diagnostics, Inc.
**Plaintiff in intervention:** United States of America

## Summary of the relator's complaint (unsealed August 21, 2026)

- **Count I (false claims, medical necessity).** Corvane billed Medicare for the full 84-gene OncoTrace panel for patients who did not meet Medicare coverage criteria, including patients with no personal or family history that would justify the panel. The relator alleges about 41,200 claims from January 2019 to December 2025 with **single damages of US$96.4 million**.
- **Count II (kickbacks).** Corvane paid US$3.2 million in "speaker fees" from 2019 to 2025 to 61 physicians who were among its highest-volume ordering physicians, to induce referrals. Claims resulting from those referrals are alleged to be false under the Anti-Kickback Statute.
- **Count III (retaliation).** The relator alleges she was demoted and then terminated in 2023 after raising concerns.

Under the False Claims Act, the government can recover three times its damages plus a civil penalty for each false claim. Defendants found liable can also face exclusion from Medicare and Medicaid, and settlements often include a Corporate Integrity Agreement with the HHS Office of Inspector General.
