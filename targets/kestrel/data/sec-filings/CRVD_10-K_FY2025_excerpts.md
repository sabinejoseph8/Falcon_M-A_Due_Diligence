## Item 1. Business

### Overview

Corvane is a clinical laboratory company. We operate seven CLIA-certified, CAP-accredited laboratories in North Carolina, Tennessee and Georgia, including the Pell Ridge Pathology laboratory we acquired in May 2025 for US$31.0 million in cash.

| Segment | FY2025 revenue (US$ millions) | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical testing | 314.8 | 73.0% |
| Biopharma services | 98.6 | 22.9% |
| Lab informatics | 17.6 | 4.1% |
| **Total** | **431.0** | **100.0%** |

### Clinical testing

Our flagship test is the **OncoTrace** hereditary cancer panel, an 84-gene test billed to Medicare under proprietary laboratory analyses (PLA) code 0419U. OncoTrace represented 44% of clinical testing revenue in FY2025. Medicare (traditional fee-for-service) accounted for 24.2% of our total revenue in FY2025.

### Payer contracts

We are in network with most large commercial health plans. Our largest commercial payer is **Orvia Health Plan**, which accounted for 11.3% of FY2025 revenue. Our network agreement with Orvia runs to December 31, 2028. Orvia may terminate the agreement on 60 days' notice if Corvane undergoes a change of control, as defined in the agreement.

Approximately 40% of our non-Medicare revenue for OncoTrace is earned under commercial contracts that set the price as a percentage of the Medicare clinical laboratory fee schedule rate for code 0419U.

### Biopharma services

We provide central laboratory, biomarker and companion diagnostic testing for clinical trials. Our largest biopharma client is **Veltrane Therapeutics, Inc.**, which accounted for 11.7% of FY2025 revenue, mostly from its ATLAS-3 Phase 3 trial of VLT-220. Our master services agreement with Veltrane allows Veltrane to terminate any work order for convenience on 90 days' notice, paying only for work performed. Biopharma services backlog was US$246.0 million at December 31, 2025.

### Suppliers

We buy most of our sequencing instruments and reagents from **Helixor Instruments**. In 2024 we signed a supply agreement with Helixor that fixes reagent prices through December 31, 2028. The prices are inclusive of all import duties and tariffs, which Helixor bears.

### Compliance program

We maintain a compliance program overseen by our Chief Compliance Officer, who reports to the Audit Committee. Our sales and marketing practices include a physician speaker program, which we believe complies with the federal Anti-Kickback Statute.

## Item 1A. Risk Factors (selected)

- **Reimbursement.** A large share of our revenue depends on Medicare rates set by CMS under the clinical laboratory fee schedule. CMS may change the rate for any code, including code 0419U, in its annual rulemaking. A reduction in the Medicare rate for OncoTrace would also reduce the price under commercial contracts that reference the Medicare rate.
- **Fraud and abuse laws.** We are subject to the federal False Claims Act, the Anti-Kickback Statute and similar state laws. Violations can lead to treble damages, per-claim penalties and exclusion from Medicare and Medicaid. Private individuals may bring "qui tam" suits under seal on behalf of the government.
- **Collections.** Payers may deny claims or recoup payments after audits. Increases in denials would reduce the amounts we collect.
- **Debt.** Our credit agreement contains a financial covenant that becomes more restrictive in 2026. See Item 7.
- **Cybersecurity.** We hold protected health information on millions of patients. A cyberattack could disrupt testing and expose us to claims and regulatory penalties.
- **Internal controls.** We have identified a material weakness in our internal control over financial reporting. See Item 9A.

## Item 3. Legal Proceedings

**Genaro Biosciences, Inc. v. Corvane Diagnostics, Inc.** (D. Del., filed April 2, 2025). Genaro alleges that the target-capture method used in OncoTrace infringes its U.S. Patent No. 11,604,318. Genaro seeks damages and an injunction. We believe the claims are without merit and intend to defend vigorously. We have not recorded an accrual because we do not believe a loss is probable and cannot estimate a range of loss.

**Hollis v. Corvane Diagnostics, Inc.** (Wake County Superior Court, N.C., filed September 2024). A putative class action by former phlebotomists alleging unpaid overtime. We have filed a motion to dismiss.

From time to time we receive inquiries and requests for information from government agencies and payers in the ordinary course of business. We are not aware of any pending government investigation that we expect to be material to our business.

## Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis (excerpts)

### Results

Total revenue grew 11.7% to US$431.0 million (FY2024: US$386.0 million). Clinical testing revenue grew 12.7% to US$314.8 million, driven by higher OncoTrace volume and **a favorable change in estimate described below**.

**Change in estimate of implicit price concessions.** In the fourth quarter of 2025, we updated the collection-rate model we use to estimate implicit price concessions for OncoTrace, based on improved collection experience for claims with dates of service in 2023 and 2024. This change in estimate increased FY2025 revenue and operating income by **US$14.2 million**, all recorded in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Adjusted EBITDA (a non-GAAP measure) was US$125.3 million, up 25.4%. See the reconciliation in our financial data.

### Liquidity and capital resources

At December 31, 2025, we had cash of US$30.8 million, US$391.8 million outstanding on our term loan and US$30.0 million drawn on our US$100.0 million revolving credit facility. The term loan matures in October 2029 and the revolving facility in October 2028.

**Financial covenant.** Our credit agreement requires that our **Consolidated Net Leverage Ratio** (consolidated total debt less unrestricted cash, divided by Consolidated EBITDA for the last four fiscal quarters) not exceed:

| Test date (quarter end) | Maximum ratio |
|---|---|
| Through June 30, 2026 | 4.50x |
| September 30, 2026 and after | 4.00x |

"Consolidated EBITDA" means operating income plus depreciation and amortization and non-cash stock-based compensation, plus restructuring charges and other non-recurring or unusual cash costs. **The total of restructuring charges and other non-recurring or unusual costs added back may not exceed 15% of Consolidated EBITDA calculated before those add-backs.** Gains on asset sales and other non-operating income are excluded. Consolidated EBITDA is therefore not the same as our Adjusted EBITDA.

The covenant is tested each quarter. A compliance certificate is due to the lenders within 45 days after each quarter end. A breach of the covenant is an event of default. After an event of default, the lenders may stop further revolving loans and accelerate all amounts outstanding. A change of control of Corvane is also an event of default. We were in compliance with the covenant at December 31, 2025.

### Outlook for FY2026

We expect FY2026 total revenue of **US$455 to 465 million** and Adjusted EBITDA of **US$134 to 140 million**.

### Critical accounting estimates: goodwill

We test goodwill for impairment annually on **October 1**. At October 1, 2025, goodwill was allocated US$241.5 million to the Clinical testing reporting unit and US$57.1 million to Biopharma services. The estimated fair value of the Clinical testing reporting unit exceeded its carrying value by approximately **14%**. Our share price was US$27.80 at the test date. Key assumptions included stable Medicare reimbursement for OncoTrace and revenue growth of 9% a year. A 10% reduction in projected cash flows of the Clinical testing unit would eliminate most of the headroom. Goodwill of US$22.3 million from the Pell Ridge acquisition was added after the test date and was not tested.

## Item 9A. Controls and Procedures (excerpt)

Management concluded that our internal control over financial reporting was **not effective** at December 31, 2025 because of a **material weakness**. The controls over the estimate of implicit price concessions for clinical testing revenue did not operate effectively. Specifically, the review of the data and assumptions used in the collection-rate model was not precise enough to detect a material misstatement. Brannock & Lyle LLP issued an adverse opinion on our internal control over financial reporting.

Our remediation plan includes hiring a revenue cycle controller, adding an independent review of model assumptions, and reconciling model inputs to cash collections each quarter. We expect to complete remediation during 2026.
