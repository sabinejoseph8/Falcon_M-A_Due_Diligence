# Seven states hold firm on 2027 PFAS ban for clothing; big retailers move to national rules

**Statehouse Wire** (state policy news service) | April 14, 2026 | By Aurelia Duquesne

Seven states with about 31% of the US population will ban the sale of new textile products, including outdoor apparel, that contain intentionally added PFAS from January 1, 2027. Lawmakers in all seven rejected industry requests this spring for a delay or for a longer exemption for "severe wet weather" gear.

The laws cover water-repellent finishes made with fluorinated chemistry, which are common on rain jackets and technical outerwear. Products already in a retailer's inventory are not exempt.

Rather than manage state-by-state rules, several large retailers have told vendors they will apply the ban nationwide. Borealis Market told brands in a March letter, reviewed by Statehouse Wire, that from January 1, 2027 it "will not accept or sell" any textile product with intentionally added PFAS anywhere in the US and that vendors must certify compliance. Summitline Sports issued a similar notice.

"Brands that planned a 2028 transition are going to be sitting on a lot of jackets they can't sell," said a sourcing consultant who advises outdoor companies.
