## Item 1. Business

Talonfield designs, builds and supports autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) for warehouses and distribution centers. We sell three things:

- **Hardware:** Talon AMR robots and charging infrastructure. Our third-generation robot (Talon Gen-3), launched in 2023, represented approximately 70% of hardware revenue in 2025.
- **Software:** FieldOS, our cloud-based fleet orchestration platform, sold as an annual or multi-year subscription. FieldOS annual recurring revenue (ARR) was $139.2 million at December 31, 2025.
- **Services:** installation, integration, maintenance and training.

In August 2024 we acquired Lumora Vision, Inc., a machine-vision software company, for $85.0 million in cash.

**Customers.** We served 276 active customers in 2025. Our largest customer, Norvale Retail Group, Inc. ("Norvale"), accounted for 31.1% of total revenue in 2025 (28.0% in 2024). Our master supply agreement with Norvale (the "Norvale MSA") runs through September 30, 2027. No other customer accounted for more than 10% of revenue.

**Suppliers.** We buy the LiDAR sensors used in all Talon AMR models from a single supplier, which manufactures them in China. We do not currently have a qualified second source.

**Employees.** We had 1,452 full-time employees at December 31, 2025.

## Item 1A. Risk Factors (selected)

**We depend on a small number of customers, and the loss of Norvale would materially harm our business.** Norvale accounted for 31.1% of our 2025 revenue and 38% of our gross accounts receivable at December 31, 2025. The Norvale MSA allows Norvale to terminate the agreement on 90 days' notice if Talonfield undergoes a change of control. Norvale is not required to purchase any minimum volume.

**We are involved in patent litigation that could require us to pay damages or stop selling products.** See Item 3. An adverse outcome could require us to pay substantial damages or royalties, redesign our products, or stop selling Talon Gen-3 robots.

**Our LiDAR supply comes from a single supplier located in China.** Changes in trade policy, including new or higher tariffs, could increase our costs or interrupt our supply.

**Cybersecurity incidents affecting FieldOS could disrupt our customers' operations.** FieldOS runs in the cloud. An outage or attack could stop customers' robot fleets, expose us to service level credits and claims, and damage our reputation.

**We identified a material weakness in our internal control over financial reporting.** See Item 9A. If we fail to remediate it, we may be unable to report our results accurately or on time.

**Our credit agreement contains financial covenants.** A breach could allow our lenders to accelerate repayment of our debt. See Note 9.

## Item 3. Legal Proceedings

*Vantor Robotics, Inc. v. Talonfield Systems, Inc.* (U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware, filed March 2024). Vantor alleges that the navigation system in our Talon Gen-3 robots infringes three of its patents and seeks damages and an injunction. We believe the claims are without merit and intend to defend ourselves vigorously. We have not recorded an accrual because we do not believe a loss is probable, and we cannot reasonably estimate a possible loss or range of loss.

*Ruiz et al. v. Talonfield Field Services LLC* (Superior Court of California, filed February 2025). A putative class action alleging wage-and-hour violations for field installation technicians in California. In December 2025 the parties agreed in principle to settle for $3.2 million, which we accrued at December 31, 2025. The settlement is subject to court approval.

From time to time we are involved in other legal proceedings arising in the ordinary course of business. We do not believe any of them are material.

## Item 9A. Controls and Procedures

Management concluded that our internal control over financial reporting was not effective as of December 31, 2025, because of a material weakness. We did not design and maintain effective controls over the review of hardware contracts with non-standard delivery and acceptance terms, including bill-and-hold arrangements. This material weakness was first identified in the third quarter of 2025. We have begun remediation, including hiring a director of technical accounting and creating a contract review committee. Linden Barr LLP issued an adverse opinion on the effectiveness of our internal control over financial reporting.

## Notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements (selected)

### Note 2. Revenue Recognition (excerpt)

Hardware revenue is recognized when control transfers to the customer, generally on shipment or delivery. In some cases a customer asks us to hold finished products at our facilities until the customer's site is ready (a "bill-and-hold" arrangement). We recognize revenue under a bill-and-hold arrangement when (a) the reason for the arrangement is substantive, (b) the products are separately identified as belonging to the customer, (c) the products are ready for physical transfer, and (d) we cannot use the products or direct them to another customer.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 we recognized $31.8 million of hardware revenue under bill-and-hold arrangements (2024: none material). Of this amount, $24.6 million related to our largest customer. At December 31, 2025, these products remained in our warehouses.

**Financial covenant.** We must maintain a Total Net Leverage Ratio of no more than 3.50 to 1.00 for each fiscal quarter ending on or before June 30, 2026, and no more than 3.00 to 1.00 for each fiscal quarter ending on or after September 30, 2026. The ratio is tested quarterly on a trailing twelve-month basis.

**Definitions (summarized from the credit agreement):**

- *Total Net Leverage Ratio* = (Total Debt minus unrestricted cash, with cash netting capped at $25.0 million) divided by Consolidated EBITDA.
- *Consolidated EBITDA* = net income plus interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, plus non-cash stock-based compensation, plus restructuring, integration and other non-recurring charges ("Permitted Add-backs"). Permitted Add-backs may not exceed 15% of Consolidated EBITDA calculated before including those Permitted Add-backs.
- A change of control of Talonfield is an event of default, which allows the lenders to demand immediate repayment of all outstanding loans.

We were in compliance with all covenants at December 31, 2025.
