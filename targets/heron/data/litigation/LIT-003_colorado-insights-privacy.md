## Claims

1. Deceptive trade practices under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act: describing Corvane Insights data as "de-identified" when it can reasonably be linked to individual households.
2. Violations of the Colorado Privacy Act: processing and selling personal data, including inferences about when a home is occupied, without valid notice and consent.

## Main allegations (allegations only)

- About 96,000 Colorado households were enrolled in Insights through an update to the Corvane Care terms of service, with an opt-out setting in the app. Fewer than 2% opted out.
- From January 2026, Corvane delivered daily occupancy indices at the ZIP+4 level to an insurer and two real-estate analytics firms.
- The Attorney General's office states that it has shared information with the Federal Trade Commission, "which is conducting its own investigation of Corvane's compliance with its 2022 consent order".

## Relief sought

A permanent injunction stopping the sale of Insights data from Colorado households without opt-in consent; deletion of data already shared; civil penalties of up to US$20,000 per violation; disgorgement; costs.
