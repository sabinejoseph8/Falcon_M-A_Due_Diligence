## Claims

Breach of express and implied warranty; violation of the Illinois Consumer Fraud Act and similar state laws; negligence; unjust enrichment. Three individual property damage suits, including one by the owners of a Westerville, Ohio home damaged by fire in December 2025, were consolidated with the class action for pre-trial purposes.

## Main allegations (allegations only, not findings)

- The backup lithium coin battery in AT-200 and AT-210 units overheats when the thermostat draws power from certain older furnace control circuits.
- Plaintiffs allege that Delmora engineers logged 58 overheating incidents in an internal "Thermal Event Tracker" by the end of 2024, more than a year before the company told investors in February 2026 that it had received "a limited number of consumer reports". The allegation relies on a declaration from a former Delmora quality engineer.
- The battery isolation kit requires owners to open the thermostat and is "a do-it-yourself fix for a fire hazard".

## Relief sought

Full refund of the purchase price for every class member, or free replacement; damages for property loss; punitive damages; attorneys' fees. The complaint does not state a total amount.

## Insurance

On August 12, 2026, Delmora's product liability insurer, Harborline Specialty Insurance Company, acknowledged notice of the claims **with a full reservation of rights**, citing the terms of the policy period that began March 1, 2026.

## Defendant's position

Delmora argues that the isolation kit fully addresses the hazard, that most class members have suffered no injury, and that warranty claims are barred by the two-year warranty period.
