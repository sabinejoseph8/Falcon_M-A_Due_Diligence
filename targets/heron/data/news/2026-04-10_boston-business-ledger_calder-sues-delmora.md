# Calder Institute sues Delmora, claims it owns thermostat AI patents

**Boston Business Ledger** | April 10, 2026

The Calder Institute of Technology has sued Delmora Home Systems and its co-founder, Dr. Anselm Reyes-Barrow, claiming that the patents behind Delmora's AdaptiveComfort thermostat algorithm belong to the Institute.

The complaint, filed on Thursday in Massachusetts Superior Court, says Reyes-Barrow developed the algorithm while working as a postdoctoral fellow in Calder's Building Energy Laboratory, using Institute computers, data and a US Department of Energy research grant. Calder's intellectual property policy, which every researcher signs, assigns to the Institute inventions "conceived or first reduced to practice" in the course of Institute research or with significant use of Institute resources.

Calder cites a laboratory notebook entry from February 2017 and an internal seminar in March 2017 at which, it says, Reyes-Barrow presented "the core of what became AdaptiveComfort".

A Delmora spokesperson said the claims were "without merit" and that AdaptiveComfort was "conceived and developed after Dr. Reyes-Barrow left Calder in June 2017".

Calder asks the court to declare it the owner of the six US patents, order their assignment, and award royalties on Delmora's thermostat sales. A university technology-transfer lawyer not involved in the case said ownership disputes of this kind "can turn on a single notebook page".
