# Your thermostat knows when you are home. Insurers are buying that.

**The Ledger Investigates (non-profit newsroom)** | August 18, 2026

Corvane Home Systems describes its Insights product as "aggregated and de-identified". A review by The Ledger found that data sold to at least one insurer can be traced back to individual homes.

The Ledger obtained a sample of Insights data delivered to Bellmark Mutual Insurance in May 2026. The data gives a daily "occupancy index" and "comfort index" for each ZIP+4 area, the postal unit that often covers a single side of a street. In the sample, the median ZIP+4 area held nine Corvane homes, and 18% held only one or two.

Working with two university privacy researchers, The Ledger matched the sample with public property records and identified the specific household behind 61% of the records in a test area. The data showed, day by day, when those homes were empty.

Bellmark confirmed it uses Insights data "as one input" in pricing homeowners insurance. Corvane said customers "agree to Insights when they accept the Corvane Care terms" and "can opt out at any time".

Corvane has operated under a Federal Trade Commission consent order since 2022, after it shared thermostat data with an advertising partner. A former FTC attorney who reviewed Corvane's enrollment screens for The Ledger said the order's requirement of "affirmative express consent" means "a clear opt-in, not a clause in updated terms of service with an opt-out switch". Two people familiar with the matter said FTC staff had contacted Corvane about Insights in July. The FTC declined to comment.
