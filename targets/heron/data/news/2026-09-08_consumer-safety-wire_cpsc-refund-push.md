# CPSC staff push Corvane to offer refunds on 1.6 million Aura thermostats

**Consumer Safety Wire** | September 8, 2026

Staff of the Consumer Product Safety Commission have made a preliminary determination that Corvane Aura thermostats, models AT-200 and AT-210, present a substantial product hazard, according to a letter to the company seen by Consumer Safety Wire.

The letter, dated August 28, says Corvane's battery isolation kit, mailed to owners since April, "does not adequately address the hazard" because owners must open the thermostat to fit it. The staff cite Corvane data showing that only 23% of kits sent had been installed by July 31.

Staff ask Corvane to agree to a recall offering owners **a full refund of the purchase price or a free replacement thermostat**, at the owner's choice. About 1.62 million affected units were sold, at an **average retail price of $119**, according to the letter. If Corvane does not agree, staff may recommend that the Commission file an administrative complaint to compel a recall.

The letter lists 296 reports of overheating, including 52 fires and 11 burn injuries, up from the figures Corvane disclosed in August.

Corvane said it "disagrees with the staff's preliminary view" and believes the kit "fully addresses the issue". The company said it would continue to work with the CPSC.
