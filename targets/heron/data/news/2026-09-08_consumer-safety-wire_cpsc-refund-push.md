# CPSC staff push Delmora to offer refunds on 1.6 million Aura thermostats

**Consumer Safety Wire** | September 8, 2026

Staff of the Consumer Product Safety Commission have made a preliminary determination that Delmora Aura thermostats, models AT-200 and AT-210, present a substantial product hazard, according to a letter to the company seen by Consumer Safety Wire.

The letter, dated August 28, says Delmora's battery isolation kit, mailed to owners since April, "does not adequately address the hazard" because owners must open the thermostat to fit it. The staff cite Delmora data showing that only 23% of kits sent had been installed by July 31.

Staff ask Delmora to agree to a recall offering owners **a full refund of the purchase price or a free replacement thermostat**, at the owner's choice. About 1.62 million affected units were sold, at an **average retail price of $119**, according to the letter. If Delmora does not agree, staff may recommend that the Commission file an administrative complaint to compel a recall.

The letter lists 296 reports of overheating, including 52 fires and 11 burn injuries, up from the figures Delmora disclosed in August.

Delmora said it "disagrees with the staff's preliminary view" and believes the kit "fully addresses the issue". The company said it would continue to work with the CPSC.
