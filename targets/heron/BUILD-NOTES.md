# Project Heron: build notes

Heron is the third fictional diligence target. Its job is to be a stricter generalization test than Kestrel: different industry, different deal type, and issue types the system was never tuned on.

## What it is

- **Deal:** Meridale Capital Partners Fund IV, a private equity fund, proposes to take private Corvane Home Systems, Inc. (NASDAQ: CVHS), a US maker of connected home climate devices (smart thermostats, air quality monitors, hubs) with a subscription and data business and a utility programs business. US$24.50 per share; enterprise value US$1,543.4 million; 11.8x LTM Adjusted EBITDA.
- **Industry and deal type:** consumer connected devices and data, sponsor take-private with a leveraged financing. Falcon and Kestrel are strategic acquisitions in other sectors.
- **Data room:** deal brief with six Investment Committee questions and a README; five SEC filing excerpts (10-K FY2025, 10-Q Q2 2026, two 8-Ks, DEF 14A); 14 news articles, including an anonymous-source piece, an activist letter, an interested-party blog, a forum post and unrelated noise; a docket index of 7 matters with 4 case files; and the eight financial files with the same names and column headers as Falcon's.
- **Answer key:** `evaluation/answer-key.md`, with 18 planted issues (4 Critical, 9 High, 4 Medium, 1 Low by first-listed severity), one planted-positives row (P19) and four distractors. Twelve of the planted issues need more than one source to find or size properly.

## How it was built

- Built blind. I read only `tests/_common.py`, `tests/test_phase2_data.py`, `tests/run_all.py`, the header lines of Falcon's financial CSVs and docket index, and the short `targets/` layout hand-over note in the shared notes folder. I did not open Falcon's or Kestrel's data or answer keys, `docs/`, `Project Memory/`, the agent or command definitions, or any earlier run.
- One caveat: the app loaded the project's `CLAUDE.md` into my context automatically at the start of the session, so I saw the lead agent's general instructions without opening the file. I chose the issue types from the brief I was given (areas the system was never pointed at) and did not use those instructions to place evidence.
- The financial statements were generated by a small script so every statement ties out. Working-capital lines in the cash flow come from the balance sheet movements, the cash balance comes from the cash flow, and equity balances the sheet. The narrative documents were then written around those numbers, and every number quoted in a document or the answer key was recomputed from the CSVs.
- Writing straight into `targets/heron/data/` is blocked for Claude's file tools by the project's permission rules (they protect source data during a run). I wrote the files in my scratchpad and copied them in with a shell command, so the guard stays as it is.

## How it was checked

- `python3 tests/run_all.py --write-manifest --target heron` wrote `data_manifest.json` (checksums of the data and the answer key).
- `python3 tests/run_all.py --phase 2` passes for every target with a manifest: balance sheets balance, income statements add up, cash flows tie to the change in cash, Adjusted EBITDA reconciles (including the detail rows), quarters sum to the years and half-years, every Markdown document carries a FICTIONAL banner, docket detail files exist, answer key IDs run without gaps, and the protected files match the manifest.
- No em dashes in any Heron file.
