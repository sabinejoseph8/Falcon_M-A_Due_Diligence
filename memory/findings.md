### X-01: Bill-and-hold sales inflated FY2025 growth

- **Finding:** Talonfield recognized US$31.8 million of hardware revenue in Q4 2025 for robots that were still in its own warehouses at year-end. Applying that filed amount to the quarterly data shows underlying FY2025 growth of about 11.6% (reported 19.1%) and underlying Q4 growth of about 5.1% (reported 32.4%). Without the bill-and-hold sales, the "record" Q4 would have missed analyst consensus. Part of the amount (US$9.4 million, 29.6%) has already been returned.
- **Evidence (combined):**
  - Filed amount: US$31.8 million of Q4 2025 bill-and-hold revenue, US$24.6 million (77.4%) from the largest customer; products "remained in our warehouses" at December 31, 2025 (10-K Note 2; SEC-02).
  - Q4 2025: 154.4 - 31.8 = 122.6 vs Q4 2024 of 116.6, so +5.1% (reported +32.4%) (calculated from 10-K and `revenue_quarterly.csv`).
  - FY2025: 502.3 - 31.8 = 470.5 vs FY2024 of 421.7, so +11.6% (reported +19.1%). Bill-and-hold is 39.5% of FY2025 growth (31.8 / 80.6). Hardware: 269.6 vs 261.9, so +2.9% (reported +15.1%) (calculated).
  - Underlying growth by quarter: Q1 2025 +13.2%, Q2 +14.4%, Q3 +14.4%, Q4 about +5.1% without bill-and-hold, Q1 2026 +0.7%, Q2 2026 -4.0%. The slowdown started in Q4 2025 and was hidden by the bill-and-hold sales (calculated from FIN-01 and SEC-02).
  - Consensus: Q4 2025 consensus was about US$128.0 million (Capital Markets Wire, March 10, 2026; NEWS-08). Reported beat was 26.4. Without bill-and-hold, Q4 revenue of 122.6 is a miss of about 5.4, or 4.2% (calculated).
  - Balance sheet signals: receivables plus contract assets rose 60.0 in FY2025; DSO went from 63.5 to 86.4 days; the credit loss allowance was cut from 3.8% to 2.1% of gross receivables; operating cash flow fell from 40.8 to 9.7 (FIN-06, FIN-08, SEC-10).
  - Reversal: US$9.4 million was returned and credited in May 2026, booked as a Q2 2026 revenue reduction rather than a 2025 correction; the other US$22.4 million was delivered by June 30, 2026 (10-Q; SEC-02, SEC-06, NEWS-04).
  - Scale check: at FY2025 hardware revenue per robot shipped of about US$70.1 thousand (FIN-02), US$31.8 million is roughly 450 robots, consistent with the short seller's "hundreds" of robots (calculated, approximate; the short seller claim is an allegation, NEWS-03).
  - Valuation base: LTM revenue to June 30, 2026 (498.4) is not distorted, because the Q4 2025 bill-and-hold (+31.8) and the Q2 2026 credit (-9.4) both fall inside the LTM window. The net 22.4 matches the robots actually delivered. FY2025 is the distorted base; LTM and forward figures are cleaner (calculated).
  - Reasons conflict: the 10-K says bill-and-hold happens when "a customer asks us to hold finished products ... until the customer's site is ready", but the 10-Q blames weak Q2 hardware on "orders that customers pulled forward". The four 10-K criteria do not require a customer request, and no filing says customers asked (SEC-28). LTM revenue excluding the bill-and-hold units entirely grew 6.0% (FIN-18).
  - Corroboration from other sources: the outgoing auditor warned in Q3 2025 about arrangements with these terms "expected in the fourth quarter of 2025" (8-K; SEC-04); the securities complaint alleges improper bill-and-hold sales "including sales the customer had not requested" (allegation; LIT-05); anonymous sources say the former CFO disputed booking Q4 orders before delivery (rumor; NEWS-06).
